Neil Benjamin

Director of Admissions, NYC · College Admissions Strategy & Executive Functioning

A college admissions strategist and executive functioning specialist who prioritizes a deep understanding of each student—how they think, what motivates them, and where they most need support.

Biography

Neil Benjamin is an educator, college admissions strategist, and executive functioning specialist with nearly fifteen years of experience supporting students across a wide range of learning profiles and academic stages. He holds a Bachelor of Arts from Middlebury College, where he studied Political Science with concentrations in Economics and Italian, and he went on to earn a master’s degree from Columbia University.

Neil joined Oxford Tutors after beginning his career in executive search and leadership consulting. As Director of Admissions in New York City, he has advised more than one hundred students and has over a decade of experience guiding applicants through highly selective college admissions. His students have earned admission to institutions including Stanford, Dartmouth, Brown, Vanderbilt, and the University of Chicago. In addition to admissions strategy and application writing, Neil specializes in executive functioning, developing individualized systems that help students strengthen organization, time management, self-advocacy, and independent learning.

A former collegiate athlete and certified mindfulness instructor for corporate clients such as Nike, Hermès, and Capital Group, Neil brings a holistic perspective to his work with students, emphasizing not only academic achievement but also confidence, resilience, healthy habits, and sustainable approaches to success. When not teaching, Neil enjoys writing, cooking, CrossFit, and film.

Teaching philosophy

I believe the most effective teaching begins with understanding the individual student: how they think, what motivates them, where they feel confident, and where they may need additional structure or support. My first priority is therefore to build a relationship grounded in trust, curiosity, and mutual respect. I want students to feel ownership and agency over their learning, with our work together shaped collaboratively rather than prescribed to them.

Because no two students learn in precisely the same way, I resist one-size-fits-all approaches. My work always starts with a “Why?” Why does a student want to achieve what they’ve set out to do, and what makes that goal meaningful to them? From there, we iterate on the “How?”—working collaboratively to identify the strategies, systems, and habits that best support how they learn. For some students, that may mean breaking a large assignment into concrete, manageable steps; for others, it may mean developing stronger organizational systems, talking through an idea before committing it to paper, or learning techniques for managing stress and sustaining attention. My background in executive functioning and mindfulness particularly informs this individualized approach.

I also believe strongly in balancing support with independence. Whether I am helping a student develop an essay, prepare for an interview, navigate the college process, or strengthen executive functioning skills, my goal is not simply to help them complete the task in front of them. It is to help them understand how they completed it successfully so they can increasingly apply those skills on their own.

Families working with me can expect warmth, transparency, organization, and clear communication. They can also expect me to hold students to high standards while remaining attentive to the very real pressures young people face. Academic success should not require sacrificing well-being.

Ultimately, I believe students thrive when they feel both supported and capable: when expectations are clear, challenges feel manageable, and they can recognize their own capacity for growth. My aim is to give students practical tools for succeeding academically while helping them develop the confidence, self-awareness, and resilience to carry those tools well beyond our work together.
