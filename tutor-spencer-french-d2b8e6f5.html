Dr. Spencer French

Writer, College Admissions Tutor & Professor of English

A writer and professor of English who works intensively with students on admissions and academic support founded on their passions.

Biography

Dr. Spencer Clark French, who holds a Doctor of Philosophy and a Master of Arts from the University of Notre Dame and a Master of Arts in Religion from Yale University, is a writer, college admissions tutor, and professor of English on postdoctoral fellowship at the University of Notre Dame. He has taught university-level English literature and writing courses. While he enjoys the classroom, he has a deep appreciation for individualized teaching in the humanities, whether that’s reading through the classics together, creating a personalized plan for academic writing support, or providing line-by-line feedback on creative writing. For his own creative work, he has won many awards, including the prestigious Frederick Buechner Prize for creative non-fiction at Yale Divinity School, selected by Christian Wiman.

Spencer has served for several cycles as an Oxford Tutors admissions consultant. For undergraduate applications, his students have been accepted at universities ranging from Yale, Cornell, the University of Chicago, and Duke to Oxford. He also consults on graduate and doctoral admissions at programs ranging from medical school, law school, and business school to divinity school and doctoral programs, with acceptances at Harvard, Yale, Princeton, Columbia, the University of Chicago, New York University, and Northwestern.

Beyond the résumé, Spencer loves to study Spanish, dance salsa and bachata, garden, and volunteer as an English as a Second Language teacher for recently immigrated neighbors in the midwestern metropolis of South Bend, Indiana.

Teaching philosophy

In our age of artificial intelligence and large classrooms, it is rare for young students to have the opportunity for personalized, one-on-one education, especially with regard to reading and writing. I believe that these two skills are essential to developing one’s critical thinking abilities, which is why our current literacy crisis is so troubling. My approach doesn’t ignore these concerns, but offers tangible, time-tested strategies for correcting them. Whether it’s trekking through Dante together, polishing a personal statement, or developing an original short story, my teaching method aims to create on-ramps for learning that is at once targeted for students’ needs and relevant to their interests. I start with their passions and go from there.

When working on long-term writing support, for example, I don’t begin with complex syntax and semicolon usage. I lead with reflective essays about their own lives and ideas, and then, when mistakes occur or opportunities for improvement arise, I offer mini lessons. Strikingly, again and again, these prompts end up becoming the groundwork of effective college essays and the applications I help students shape through the admissions process. I also believe that this kind of personalized tutoring must come within a wider community of support, which is why I write session reports after every meeting and make space in my schedule for parent meetings; it really does take a village.

I love tutoring not simply as a means to the end of sending students off to their great college adventures, though, of course, that is quite gratifying. I love even more the process of negotiating the trenches of the slow, sometimes uncomfortable process of learning: encouraging students in some moments, creating space for them to work out problems for themselves in others. Reading and writing are two of the most essential skills young people can acquire in this life, and I count myself lucky to be just one more support in that long journey of learning.
