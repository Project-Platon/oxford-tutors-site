Benjamin Ruskin

Educational Consultant · Test Preparation, Mathematics, Writing & Executive Functioning

A calm, organized coach who excels at helping students turn ambitious goals into manageable next steps.

Biography

Benjamin Ruskin is an educational consultant and tutor with Oxford Tutors USA, where he works with students on executive functioning, academics, standardized test preparation, mathematics, writing, and college admissions essays. He brings an interdisciplinary background to his tutoring, combining quantitative training in economics, legal writing experience, and graduate study in mental health.

Ben graduated cum laude from Haverford College with a degree in economics, earned his Juris Doctor from the University of California, Los Angeles School of Law, and is currently pursuing a Master of Arts in Marriage and Family Therapy at Syracuse University. At the University of California, Los Angeles School of Law, he graduated with Order of the Coif honors, was selected by faculty to serve as a Legal Research and Writing tutor for first-year law students, and served as Co-Editor-in-Chief of the Disability Law Journal, the only disability law journal in the nation. He later clerked for Chief Judge Nannette Jolivette Brown in the United States District Court for the Eastern District of Louisiana and practiced litigation and employment law in Los Angeles.

A barred attorney, Ben returned to tutoring after realizing that his greatest professional passion was helping students and families navigate academic challenges with more confidence, structure, and clarity. His work often focuses on test strategy, writing, mathematics, organization, time management, and supporting bright students who feel overwhelmed by the demands in front of them. Outside of tutoring, Ben is a former college golfer who still plays competitively in his free time.

Teaching philosophy

My teaching philosophy begins with the belief that students do their best work when they feel understood, respected, and given a clear path forward. Many students are not struggling because they lack ability. They are struggling because the task feels too large, the instructions feel unclear, the timeline feels overwhelming, or past frustration has made it harder to begin. My role is to help translate that uncertainty into a concrete next step.

I build relationships with students by being warm, direct, and nonjudgmental. I want students to feel comfortable being honest about what they understand, what they avoided, what they forgot, and what still feels difficult. Once a student feels safe being candid, we can usually make progress quickly because we are working with the real problem rather than only the surface-level assignment.

I adapt my approach to the student in front of me. Some students need structure, accountability, and a specific plan. Others need space to think aloud, test ideas, and build confidence before committing to an answer. In test preparation, I focus on process, pattern recognition, and deliberate review rather than shortcuts. In writing, I help students clarify what they are trying to say, then build the structure and language to say it well. In mathematics, I emphasize step-by-step reasoning, mistake patterns, and helping students understand why a method works rather than simply memorizing procedures. In executive-function work, I try to create systems that are simple enough to be used consistently.

Families can expect me to be collaborative, practical, and attentive to the student’s broader context. I care not only about whether a student completes the immediate assignment, but also whether they are building the skills, confidence, and self-awareness to handle the next challenge more independently.
