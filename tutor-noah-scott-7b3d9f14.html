Noah Scott

Educator · Mathematics, Science, Test Preparation & Executive Functioning

A versatile educator who excels at building rapport with students, helping them succeed by ensuring that they feel seen and heard for who they are.

Biography

I have been an educator, teaching and tutoring, for five years. I mainly work with students from kindergarten through twelfth grade, but have also worked with students in college and beyond.

I specialize in mathematics and science, test preparation for the SAT, ACT, ISEE, and SSAT, and general executive-function coaching. I also have experience tutoring English and language arts, history, Spanish, and the college admissions process. I am fluent in English and Spanish, and have tutored children in both languages.

For my undergraduate degree, I studied both acting and physics at Southern Methodist University in Dallas, Texas, and conducted research in the areas of particle physics and helicopter innovation. I am currently in the middle of my Master’s in International Education at Universidad Camilo José Cela in Madrid. In between my undergraduate and graduate studies, I lived in the Canary Islands for a year as a Fulbright Scholar, teaching English to children from kindergarten through twelfth grade.

I am originally from Austin, Texas, and am currently based in New York City.

Teaching philosophy

My teaching style varies from student to student based on my personal assessment of each student’s learning style and their social and emotional needs.

Overall, I am a big believer in positive reinforcement and Montessori-style teaching: of letting students find their own approach to schoolwork and studying, with me offering suggestions and resources as they go. And in the moments where things don’t go their way, I work to be a kind and encouraging presence, allowing students the space to regroup, analyze their mistakes, and find a new approach.

Within this style, I also make sure to be realistic about the deadlines and benchmarks that students need to meet. I value open and honest communication with my students. If a benchmark is not being hit, or if it looks like we may not get there in time, we will review the student’s goals and find ways to rework our general strategy in accordance.

What I love most about tutoring is helping students to feel empowered for who they are as an individual. Much of this happens little by little as we work, through conversations about each student’s likes and dislikes, their social and academic lives, the way they learn best, and their sources of inspiration. I find that when students are working, or even speaking, from a place of inspiration and motivation, they are more open to completing difficult assignments, taking in new information, and trying out new ways of thinking and working. I believe that even within the world of assignments and deadlines, letting students feel seen and heard for who they are gives them more motivation to be active participants in shaping the world.
