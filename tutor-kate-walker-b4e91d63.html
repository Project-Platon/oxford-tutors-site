Kate Walker

Writing Tutor & College Admissions Advisor · Research, Argument & the Humanities

A Yale Divinity scholar and editor whose Socratic approach helps students turn curiosity into focused research.

Biography

Kate Walker is a current Master of Arts in Religion student at Yale Divinity School (’27) with a concentration in Women’s, Gender, & Sexuality Studies in Religion. She holds an undergraduate degree from Gordon College, where she majored in Philosophy and Biblical Studies and minored in Psychology and Theology. At Gordon, Kate led writing workshops for student reporters while serving as the Editor in Chief of the Tartan, Gordon’s official student newspaper. She spent her senior year studying abroad at the University of Oxford, where her writing and confidence benefitted immensely from the tutorial model.

She was awarded the Baker Academic Award for Theology and Departmental Honors in Biblical Studies for her undergraduate thesis, titled “Fear of Woman, Fear of God: Julian of Norwich, Margery Kempe, and the ‘Grotesque Mystical Body’ in Medieval England.” As a staff member of East Rock Institute, the nation’s oldest non-profit organization devoted to Korean and Korean American culture, she guided high school and undergraduate-aged summer interns to conduct original research from the rare archives stored at ERI.

She has edited over thirty in-print books as an Editorial Assistant for Hendrickson Publishers and has edited manuscripts and articles for four professors between Gordon and Yale. Currently, she is working at the Yale Divinity Library and is conducting her research amongst the intersections of Asian American history, religion, enslavement, gender, sexuality, and class.

Teaching philosophy

My tutoring style is fundamentally Socratic. I often find that the most creative projects emerge from asking students open-ended questions and seeing where their minds take them. As part of my tutoring practice, I ask students to make a conscious effort to observe the world around them. Whenever something prompts them to think, “That’s a weird way of doing things,” I ask them to reflect on what is working well and what isn’t. What is the story being told, and what seems to be missing? This practice, even on the smallest scale, is the beginning of research with direction. It teaches students to identify real/potential issues with the status quo and to brainstorm improvements based on their own knowledge and experiences. It is especially helpful for students who tend to feel immobilized by perfectionism or anxiety because it helps students to understand themselves better and what they have to offer.

In a similar vein, I will always work from what a student brings to a tutoring session without judgment, whether it be a final draft or a blank page. Everyone experiences ups and downs in life, and there are weeks that we worry that we have accomplished absolutely nothing at all—which is never the case! I encourage students to show up to tutoring sessions regardless of how they felt their week went. We can have a low-pressure conversation about what might be stressing them out and make a plan for the next week. Depending on a student’s learning style, I might suggest setting time blocks, finding ways to minimize friction between tasks, teaching a difficult concept to someone else, or creating a list of incredibly attainable goals to check off of a list—all tips that I’ve implemented into my own writing process.

My philosophy, summarized, is that pretty much anything in the world can be learned if one knows where to look. My job is to help students learn the foundations of thinking critically, finding and analyzing sources, developing an argument, and defending it, having considered alternate perspectives. In the process, we will be able to discern between students’ interests that show up primarily for their resumes and the ones that bring energy and joy to their lives. I hope to provide a welcoming environment for students to test their ideas, learn more about themselves, and develop foundational skills for any subject they hope to pursue in the future.
