A Mind That Races Ahead of Its Feelings

A French study of 328 children found gifted pupils ahead on creativity, school engagement, and motivation, but only at the pace of their peers on social and emotional development.

Giftedness · Oxford Tutors · 5 minute read

What the research found

Parents of gifted children often describe a particular kind of mismatch. The child reasons like an adult, then melts down like a much younger one. There is a name for this in the research literature, asynchronous development, and this study set out to see whether it shows up when you measure different parts of a child at once.

The researchers studied 328 children in France, of whom 45 were identified as gifted, and measured three quite different things. One was abstract graphic creativity, a test of inventive visual thinking. Another was how the children felt about school. The third was their engagement in the school environment. The design let the researchers ask not just whether gifted children were ahead, but whether they were ahead evenly, across every dimension, or unevenly, racing in some areas while keeping pace in others.

On the cognitive and motivational side, the gifted children stood out. They scored significantly higher than their non-gifted peers on self-determination, on their feelings about school, and on creativity. By these measures, giftedness looked like an advantage, and a broad one.

But the study found the advantage was lopsided. The gifted children showed markedly higher scores on motivation and intellectual skills, yet their socio-affective development, the social and emotional side, sat at ordinary levels for their age. The authors read this as a clear instance of asynchronous development: the cognitive and motivational sphere had run ahead while the socio-affective sphere had not. The mind was racing; the feelings were keeping to the calendar.

It is worth being precise about what this design can and cannot show. This is a cross-sectional study, a single snapshot rather than a film of children growing. It captures a gap between cognitive and emotional development at one moment; it cannot show that gap widening or closing over time. The gifted group was small, just 45 children, which makes the findings suggestive rather than definitive. And this is one French sample, using particular instruments, so the exact pattern may not repeat everywhere.

What it means for you and your child

If you have ever felt whiplash watching your gifted child move from a startlingly mature argument to a very ordinary tantrum, this study offers a framework for it. The two things are not a contradiction, and they are not a sign that something is wrong. A child can be genuinely ahead in reasoning and creativity while being exactly their age emotionally. The advanced mind and the age-typical heart live in the same body.

The practical value of holding this in mind is that it guards against a common error: reading a child's intellectual maturity as emotional maturity, and expecting the feelings to keep up with the reasoning. A ten-year-old who discusses ideas like a fifteen-year-old is still, in the ways that matter for disappointment and frustration and friendship, a ten-year-old. Expecting otherwise sets both parent and child up to feel that the emotional side is somehow failing, when it is simply developing on its own schedule.

There is a gentler reading, too, in the finding that gifted children felt more positive about school and were more engaged. That is worth savouring. It cuts against the image of the bored, alienated gifted pupil. For many able children, school is a place they are glad to be. The task for the adults around them is less to manufacture engagement than to make sure the emotional support keeps pace with the intellectual stretch.

We would offer the same caution we always do about a single study, and here especially, given the small gifted sample. This is one snapshot of one group of French children. Its contribution is to give a familiar parental observation a name and a little evidence, not to settle how asynchrony works. Read alongside the wider literature on gifted development, it supports a humane and steadying idea. On its own, it is a reason to hold your child's mind and their feelings to different clocks, not a formula for either.

Children (Basel), 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36662132/ This essay is written for families; the paper itself is the fuller, technical account.
