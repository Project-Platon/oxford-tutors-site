Jay Ben Markson

Director of Academics & Senior Tutor · Executive Function, Admissions & Humanities

A sensitive, process-oriented mentor who strives to foster not only immediate improvement but long-term intellectual and emotional fulfillment.

Biography

Jay Ben Markson is the Director of Academics at Oxford Tutors as well as a Senior Tutor. He has worked with Oxford Tutors since 2017, specializing in executive function mentorship, college admissions support, writing and humanities academic tutoring, and counseling for neurodivergent students and their families.

Jay Ben co-designed Oxford Tutors’ approach to executive function mentorship, which is founded on a process-over-product philosophy that helps students develop intrinsic motivation. He is also the author of The Common Sense Guide to the SAT, an approachable introduction to the standardized test.

After receiving his Bachelor of Arts from Dartmouth College in Theater and Poetry, Jay Ben earned his Master of Fine Arts from Case Western Reserve University. He also served as Associate Artistic Director of MaineStage Shakespeare, a nonprofit classical theater with a strong educational component, for five years. He now lives outside of Boston with his wife and their rescue pit mix.

Teaching philosophy

I pride myself on an approach to student mentorship that is Socratic, sensitive, process-oriented, and designed to foster not only immediate improvement but long-term intellectual and emotional fulfillment.

When I begin working with a student, my first goal is to start building up their sense of trust and ownership in our work together. This involves asking a lot of questions. The purpose of these questions is often not to produce a specific response, but to prompt the student to think, thereby developing their ability to discern solutions on their own. At the same time, especially in the first few sessions, we quickly implement a set of standard procedures and tools to help the student keep track of the work we’re doing. At a minimum, these procedures tend to include approaches to calendarization, task management, and self-reflection, but we constantly examine what works or doesn’t work. Over time, we strive to create a system that is precisely tuned to the student’s needs, one that can serve as a template for their own self-management in the future.

In my admissions work, I urge students to prize authenticity and self-knowledge above all else. The admissions process can be an opportunity to develop a deeper understanding of what we want and to refine the skills needed to express ourselves effectively to others. I support students in a highly customized, narrative-based format that results in applications that are memorable and deeply personal.
