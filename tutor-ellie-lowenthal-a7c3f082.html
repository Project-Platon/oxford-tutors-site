Ellie Lowenthal

Humanities Tutor · AP Humanities, Neurodiversity & Mental-Health Support

An Oxford-educated tutor with over a decade of one-to-one experience and an empathetic, tailored approach.

Biography

Ellie Lowenthal is an Oxford-educated tutor with over a decade of one-to-one teaching experience, specialising in an empathetic, tailored approach. She holds a Master of Arts in Geography from the University of Oxford, where she studied Earth System Sciences and Space, Place and Society, specialising in Contemporary Urban Life and Cultural Spaces and graduating with First Class honours. She also trained in professional acting at the Bristol Old Vic Theatre School.

Ellie covers a broad range of subjects across the high school curriculum, with detailed knowledge of the Humanities Advanced Placement exams, including AP English Language and Literature, AP History (World, US, and European), AP Environmental Science, and AP Human Geography. She works online, in person, and through full-day intensives. Across more than five thousand hours of one-to-one teaching, she has helped struggling students move from failing grades to passing, and from the middle of the pack to top marks.

She specialises in supporting students who are out of school for mental-health reasons, and neurodiverse students navigating executive-functioning and interpersonal challenges. She is especially suited to students drawn to the arts and to those who need a gentler approach.

Teaching philosophy

Alongside regular academic tutoring, I specialise in building long-term relationships with students who are out of school, or attending part-time, for mental health reasons. This has ranged from personally tailored lessons to maintain academics while out of school, building a curriculum to work towards returning to school, to organising activities and cultural outings to boost morale and explore interests outside of the curriculum. Within this, I work as both mentor and tutor with neurodiverse students who are navigating challenges around interpersonal skills and executive functioning.

My teaching philosophy centres on helping students understand their own brains. Once a student can recognise their own strengths and weaknesses, we can build systems together that fortify their strengths and support their weaknesses. My approach comes from a place of softness and empathy, grounded in the understanding that neurodiverse students have often been misunderstood or misinterpreted.

I frequently guide students through a “My Guide to My Brain” project, which helps them get to know the specifics of their own learning process and, crucially, articulate it to others. This can transform a student’s ability to self-advocate at school, and can often ease tensions within the family too.

Where it’s useful, I also offer full-day intensives at a student’s home and focused sessions built around a specific goal, such as exam revision, that give us the time and space to work on longer projects like “My Guide to My Brain” over the summer. A longer stretch together, beyond the confines of a single tutoring hour, is often where the most meaningful progress happens.

From a university admissions perspective, I help students find a sense of direction, sometimes a genuine vocation, and identify routes into specialist fields. I have supported students living with a wide range of conditions, including PTSD, depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder, ADHD, autism, dyslexia, dyscalculia, POTS, chronic fatigue, and other chronic physical health conditions. My role always remains educational and mentoring, frequently working alongside the clinical professionals in a student’s life.
