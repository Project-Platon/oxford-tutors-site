Strength-Based Learning: A Popular Term, Rarely Defined

The terms a school will use, and how much evidence stands behind each.

Learning Differences · Oxford Tutors · 6 minute read

What the research found

A national Joint Commission on Twice-Exceptional Learners settled on the working definition that American schools still use: a student who demonstrates the potential for high achievement or creative productivity in one or more domains, such as mathematics, science, or the arts, and who also manifests one or more disabilities as defined by federal or state eligibility criteria. Both at once, in the same student.

The trouble is in how the two present. Behaviors associated with autism overlap with behaviors associated with giftedness, and an intense, narrow absorption in one subject can be read as either. When it is read as one of them, the other frequently goes unrecorded, and an assessment built to find a deficit leaves the talent undocumented.

A five-year research program at the University of Connecticut, paid for by a federal Javits grant, set out to describe how these students succeed anyway. Sally Reis and Sara Renzulli of the Neag School of Education summarised it in the journal Behavioral Sciences in June 2025. Their article synthesises the team’s own studies rather than searching the literature systematically, so it carries no search protocol and no pooled statistics, and ends in recommendations.

Forty academically talented college students with autism were interviewed about their high school years, along with their parents, a sample of high school teachers and counselors, and ten college disability service providers. A survey reached 147 college accessibility staff.

Every student in that sample had already succeeded, which is where the evidence thins. Retrospective interviews with young people who reached competitive colleges can describe what they found useful, but with no comparison group, whether the same practices would carry a struggling student was never tested.

A companion review counted the published literature on gifted individuals with autism and found 32 articles between 1996 and 2019, of which 62.5 percent reported data. Reis and Renzulli observe that most researchers who use the phrase strength-based learning do not define it.

Some of what the interviews produced is specific enough to hold. Asked which personal trait most enabled their success in college, 26 of the 40 named a driving passion for learning. Most were strong readers and some were strong writers, while mathematics was the common difficulty, contrary to the folklore placing these students in the sciences. Every successful student could name one or two adults they felt connected to across the high school years, a teacher, a counselor or a parent.

What it means for you and your child

A school will use this vocabulary, and each term carries a different weight of evidence.

Twice-exceptional, or 2e, is the formal term, with an operational definition behind it, and it names both halves in one word. Enrichment means work that goes deeper or wider than the standard curriculum. Acceleration means the same curriculum taken earlier or faster. Differentiated instruction means the material adjusted around a particular weakness while the intellectual demand stays high. Accommodation is the legal category and travels through a formal plan. The four provisions are distinct, and hearing them used interchangeably is informative.

Strength-based pedagogy, the loosest term in the set, means in this literature teaching organized around what a student is good at and interested in, with support for the disability supplied alongside the teaching. The interviews support the principle consistently; the practices underneath it are thinly researched, which the authors say plainly about their own field.

Self-determination has a settled meaning in this research, the ability to make life decisions independently. It names what a parent watches for across the high school years, in the choosing of a course, a club, a direction. Self-advocacy is narrower, the ability to state a need to a teacher and ask for a specific adjustment, and difficulty with it appeared across every study in the program.

Anxiety ran through all of it, though the figures a parent may be handed come from other samples. The finding that 91.6 percent of children with autism met criteria for two or more anxiety disorders comes from 108 high-functioning children already enrolled in anxiety treatment trials, so it describes children referred for anxiety rather than autistic children in general. Autistic overwhelm, the participants’ own word for the state that follows too much social contact, has no diagnostic status.

In a meeting, the productive question is one of category. Asking whether a proposed measure is enrichment, acceleration, differentiation, or accommodation forces the answer into a form that can be checked at the next review, and asking which member of staff is responsible for the talent half of the file often reveals that the post has not been filled. The connection these students named ran to one or two adults, at school or at home, and it held across the whole sample; a school can be asked which member of staff occupies that place.

This synthesis cannot show that the strategies produced the outcomes, and its authors do not claim otherwise. What the vocabulary gives you is an exact name for what you have been watching for years, and a meeting conducted in a school’s own terms is considerably harder to end without an answer.
