Chase Cooper

Educator & Test-Preparation Specialist · Standardized Testing & Individualized Academic Support

A veteran test-preparation specialist who knows that effective tutoring begins with understanding the student as a whole person, not simply as a set of grades or test scores.

Biography

Chase Cooper is an experienced educator and test-preparation specialist who has worked with hundreds of clients since beginning his tutoring practice in 2014. Over more than a decade of teaching, he has helped clients achieve significant score improvements across a wide range of standardized exams, including the SAT, ACT, LSAT, GRE, GMAT, MCAT, SSAT, ISEE, SHSAT, and Advanced Placement exams. He has also traveled domestically and internationally to conduct intensive, in-person tutoring and test preparation tailored to individual needs and goals.

Chase earned his Bachelor of Arts in History from Whitman College, where he focused on twentieth-century U.S.–Latin American foreign relations and minored in Politics. He studied abroad in Muscat, Oman, focusing on Arabic and economic development, and completed additional coursework at Columbia University. He later earned his Juris Doctor, cum laude, from Fordham University School of Law. Originally from Seattle, he spent nearly a decade living and working with clients in New York City and Chicago before relocating to the Bay Area in 2024.

While he has taught a broad range of academic subjects, Chase’s practice specializes in standardized test preparation and individualized academic support. He has extensive experience with different learning profiles, including neurodiverse students, and incorporates executive-function coaching into his work. Above all, he prides himself on avoiding a one-size-fits-all approach. Rather, he prioritizes getting to know new students, identifying their strengths and areas for growth, and developing personalized strategies and timelines to help them achieve their goals.

Teaching philosophy

My teaching philosophy begins with the belief that effective tutoring requires understanding the student as a whole person, not simply as a set of grades or test scores. I build relationships with students by first understanding what they hope to accomplish and, just as importantly, identifying the obstacles that may be standing in their way. I take a genuine interest in my students—their interests, activities, personalities, ambitions, and concerns—because I have found that strong relationships and mutual trust are often essential to meaningful academic progress.

I do not believe in a one-size-fits-all approach to teaching. More than a decade of working with students of different ages, backgrounds, abilities, and learning profiles has taught me to adapt both my teaching style and strategies to the individual. When appropriate, I review neuropsychological evaluations and collaborate with parents, teachers, and other professionals to develop a more complete understanding of how a student learns. My goal is to identify not only areas of difficulty, but also the strengths we can draw upon to help each student reach their fullest potential.

Families working with me can expect engagement, responsiveness, and transparency. I believe in maintaining an ongoing dialogue about a student’s progress—what is working, what is not, and where we may need to adjust our approach. I am also candid and forthright about what is realistically achievable, whether we are discussing academic performance, standardized test scores, or a particular timeline. I would rather establish thoughtful, realistic expectations from the outset than make promises that may not serve a student or family well. Throughout my career, clients have consistently valued this candor and the trust it creates.

Ultimately, I believe students thrive when they feel understood, supported, and equipped with practical tools to overcome the barriers they face. Those barriers may be conceptual, or they may involve test anxiety, executive-functioning challenges, confidence, or self-esteem. My role is to help students understand those challenges without being defined by them, develop strategies for overcoming them, and recognize their own capabilities. The objective is not simply a better grade or test score, but a more confident, resilient, and independent individual.
