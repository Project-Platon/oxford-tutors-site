Why the classroom keeps misreading

A difficulty travels a long way before it reaches a grade.

  1. 01

    The format decides

    A classroom asks for one kind of answer, at one speed, in one medium. A mind built another way meets that format before it ever meets the material.

  2. 02

    The gift hides the gap

    An able child compensates, often brilliantly, and the effort masks the difficulty. The strength is precisely what keeps the struggle from being seen.

  3. 03

    The wrong name arrives

    What is left visible is a lower mark, a missed deadline, a flat report. Lazy, careless, and unmotivated are the words that tend to follow, and none of them is true.

  4. 04

    The child believes it

    Told often enough that the trouble is character, a student stops trusting a mind that is, in fact, working hard and working well. That is the damage worth undoing first.

How the work is built

In order, and around one child.

  1. 01

    Read the mind first

    We take in what a family already knows, and any assessment already done, before we teach a single lesson. Clinicians are welcome in the room from the start.

  2. 02

    One tutor, matched to it

    A single senior tutor, chosen for the subject and the temperament, so that trust has time to form and the teaching can be shaped to how this child learns.

  3. 03

    Teach the material

    Real thinking, writing, and mastery across subjects, taught the way it is understood at Oxford, one student and one tutor thinking aloud together.

  4. 04

    Build the machinery

    Planning, starting, and following through, taught inside the schoolwork itself rather than as a separate skill bolted beside it.

  5. 05

    Hold it together

    One point of coordination across home, school, and clinicians, and a steady hand through the transitions that unsettle a complex learner.
